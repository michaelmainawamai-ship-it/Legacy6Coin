# A Letter from the Founder

Legacy6Coin began during a period of pressure and uncertainty. I wanted to create something that could represent resilience: not the idea that hardship disappears, but that people can meet it with patience, responsibility, and the determination to continue.

The project is also a commitment to be more transparent than the noise that often surrounds digital assets. That means documenting what the contract does, describing who holds authority, publishing material decisions, and being clear about what is known, what remains under review, and what is not promised. Documentation cannot remove risk, but it can help people make better-informed decisions.

Responsible stewardship matters because people may rely on public project information when deciding whether to read, build on, or interact with a contract. I recognize that the owner retains important administrative powers, including the ability to mint within the stated supply cap. Those powers and their risks should be disclosed plainly, not hidden behind slogans. The project should also treat security reviews, careful operations, and accurate records as ongoing responsibilities.

Community trust is earned through consistent conduct, not claimed in advance. It depends on listening, correcting mistakes, making verifiable disclosures, and respecting that each person must decide independently whether to participate. Witnesses and holders should not be described as having powers that the contract does not give them.

Legacy6Coin will favor long-term credibility over short-term hype. There are no guaranteed listings, utility, liquidity, returns, or deployment outcomes. The January 14, 2027 (God willing) date is a review milestone, not a promised launch. Our responsibility is to communicate carefully and make decisions that can be examined over time.

Thank you to everyone who takes the time to read, question, review, and contribute constructively.

**Michael Wamae**  
Founder, Legacy6Coin
